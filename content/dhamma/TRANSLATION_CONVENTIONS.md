# Translation Conventions

**Internal working reference** — not cited from `glossary.md`. The glossary is intended to read as a standalone document; this file exists for us when drafting and revising entries, and should not be pointed to from the glossary itself.

Opinionated English renderings used in the glossary, with rationale and stratum-scope. Consensus-rendering terms are not listed here — they track `_references/` lexica (DPD, PED, NCPED, MW, Apte, BHSD) by default.

## Per-entry format in the glossary

When a preferred rendering diverges from the dominant English convention _and_ a reader is likely to arrive with the other rendering in mind, append a terse italic note after the gloss:

> **samādhi** (Skt: same) — composure, collectedness. _Standardly "concentration."_

Guidelines:

- **Keep it terse.** One short sentence. No pointer back to this file — rationale stays here, the glossary stays standalone.
- **Only where confusion is likely.** High-divergence renderings (samādhi, jhāna-factor terms, nimitta) warrant the note. Minor shifts (e.g. `pāmojja` = "gladness" vs. "joy") do not.
- **Don't qualify per-stratum inside the note.** The per-Part stratum annotation already scopes the rendering; the convention note is purely for the specific entry it appears on.
- **Longer def-flags get a separate italic continuation paragraph**, blank-line separated — the inline format is only for the short "Standardly X" note.

## Cluster 1: Kumāra 2022 — EBT samādhi/jhāna vocabulary

**Source:** Kumāra Bhikkhu, _What You Might Not Know about Jhāna & Samādhi_ (2022). Summary at `~/iCloud/jbkjr/BUDDHA/dhamma/media/text/dhamma/jhana/jhana_samadhi_book_summary.md`.

**Scope:** Applies to Parts II–X (EBT strata — especially III.c Jhāna & Samādhi, III.b Hindrances, VI.f Mental Factors where factor-terms recur). **Does not apply to Part XI.b** (commentarial meditation apparatus); that section preserves the commentarial reification through its own distinct vocabulary (kasiṇa, appanā-samādhi, paṭibhāga-nimitta, pañca-jhānaṅga) rather than by re-glossing III.c terms.

**Rationale summary:** The samādhi/jhāna cluster underwent semantic shift between the EBT and the Visuddhimagga-era commentaries. The dominant English renderings ("concentration," "absorption," "one-pointedness," "seclusion") track the commentarial reification — samādhi as effortful fixation on a single object, jhāna as a deep absorptive trance excluding body-awareness. Kumāra's renderings restore the EBT sense: samādhi as mental unification-through-letting-go, jhāna as a broader meditative state compatible with body-awareness and insight, with the jhāna-factor terms read as describing progressive mental composure rather than the mechanics of absorption. This is not a fringe position — it aligns with Thanissaro, Shankman, and the broader "two-jhānas" scholarly reading; Kumāra's contribution is a coherent English rendering set.

**Preferred renderings:**

| Pāli       | Preferred gloss           | Dominant English                                                                 | Include "standardly rendered" note? |
| ---------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| samatha    | settling                  | tranquillity, serenity (NOT "calm abiding" — that's Tibetan/Mahāyāna convention) | yes                                 |
| samādhi    | composure, collectedness  | concentration                                                                    | yes                                 |
| ekaggatā   | stillness                 | one-pointedness                                                                  | yes                                 |
| ekodibhāva | poise                     | unification, singleness (of mind)                                                | yes                                 |
| jhāna      | meditation (when glossed) | meditative absorption                                                            | yes (but see note below)            |
| vitakka    | thinking                  | applied thought, directed thought                                                | yes                                 |
| vicāra     | considering               | sustained thought, examination                                                   | yes                                 |
| sukha      | happiness                 | bliss, pleasure, ease                                                            | yes                                 |
| pīti       | joy                       | rapture                                                                          | yes                                 |

**Entry-specific notes:**

- `jhāna` is left untranslated as the headword (per general glossary convention — technical Pāli terms with no settled English equivalent stay in Pāli). The preferred gloss "meditation" or "meditative stage" applies only when a gloss is needed in the def body.
- `nimitta`: the Kumāra "basis / object" rendering is for general EBT usage. The commentarial three-fold series (parikamma-/uggaha-/paṭibhāga-nimitta) in XI.b retains the commentarial "sign" sense, since the three-fold progression is itself a commentarial apparatus. The III.c `nimitta` entry should cross-ref XI.b.
- `ekaggatā`: already has a def-flag in III.c noting the EBT vs. commentarial (pañca-jhānaṅga) divergence. The "stillness" rendering applies to the EBT sense; XI.b pañca-jhānaṅga can use the conventional "one-pointedness" framing where it helps clarify the commentarial reification being described.
- `vipassanā`: Kumāra prefers "clear-seeing / discernment" to undercut the samatha/vipassanā-as-separate-techniques framing. The note is optional because "insight" is so entrenched that fighting it in every entry risks more confusion than it resolves; decide per entry.
- `sukha`: the fourth jhāna's pairing with `adukkham-asukham` ("neither suffering nor happiness") is why "happiness" beats "pleasure / bliss" — the term has to cover the broader non-sensory happiness that persists into the third jhāna and just ceases at the fourth. Worth noting in the def itself.
